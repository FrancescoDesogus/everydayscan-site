Privacy policy

EverydayScan, last updated 24 October 2026

EverydayScan is a personal Android app. Its developer runs no server and receives no data from it: no account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking.

What the app stores

The PDFs you scan, their names, the folders you make and the text read from the PDFs are stored in the app's private storage on your phone.

The camera

Scanning a document or an ID card uses the document scanner of Google Play services, which runs on the phone and hands the pages to the app. Copying text shows the camera and reads the words in the picture you take, on the phone. That picture isn't saved; the text goes to the clipboard only when you tap Copy.

Text inside PDFs

The app reads the text of each PDF so the search can find it. This happens on the phone, and the text stays there: it isn't part of the Drive copy.

The scanner and the text recognition come from Google's ML Kit and run on the phone. The pictures and the text aren't sent to Google; ML Kit may send Google figures about how its features perform, as Google's ML Kit terms describe.

The fingerprint lock

A PDF can be locked so that opening or sending it asks for the fingerprint or the phone's screen lock. Android asks for it in its own window; the app only learns whether it was given, never the fingerprint or the PIN.

Google Drive

If you connect Google Drive, the app keeps a copy of your PDFs and folders in a "EverydayScan" folder of your own Drive, so they are on your other phones and survive a new one. It uses the drive.file permission, which lets it see and change only the files and folders it created itself; the rest of your Drive stays invisible to it. The copies go straight from your phone to Google; nobody else receives them.

EverydayScan's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing your data

Uninstalling the app removes the data on the phone. The Drive copy is the "EverydayScan" folder in your Drive, which you can delete at any time. You can withdraw the app's access to Drive from your Google account's connections page.
